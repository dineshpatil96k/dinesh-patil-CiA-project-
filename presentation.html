The Global Trade Shift: Impact of the Russia–Ukraine War

Howthe February 2022invasion reconfigured energy flows, commodity

markets, logistics networks, and the politics of trade — and what businesses and nations must do to adapt.

SLIDE 2

February 2022: The Sudden Shock

  • Gas price crisis
  • European gas spot prices spiked ~580% year-on-year, disrupting industry and household budgets.
  • Immediate market volatility
  • Global economic instability surfaced within days as risk premia and FX volatility surged.
  • Energy price shock
  • Brent crude rose >25% in two weeks — a direct hit to energy- importing economies.
SLIDE 3

The Commodity Crisis: Food and Energy
Scale of disruption

Russia andUkraine accountedfor roughly

36% of global wheat exports and over 50% of sunflower oil — a shock that cascaded through food supply chains and prices.

  • Global food insecurity rose: 258M people in acute need (2022) vs 193M (2021).
  • Fertilizer and energy costs pushed input prices higher for farmers worldwide.

SLIDE 5

The Sanctions Era: 2022–2023

Widespread sanctions targeted Russian banks, shipping, and key export sectors, reshaping trade corridors and prompting rapid policy responses from affected firms and governments.

  • Russian GDP contraction was notable but milder than some early forecasts.
  • Q1 2023 federal budget deficits reached ~2,400 billion roubles amid fiscal re-prioritization.
SLIDE 6

Sectoral Fallout: Autos to Agriculture

  • Automotive
  • Chip and component shortages forced production cuts and order delays across global OEMs.
  • Emerging importers
  • Countries like India experienced higher import bills for oil and key commodities, pressuring trade balances.
  • Agriculture
  • Fertilizer price spikes threatened yields and raised food import bills for vulnerable countries.
SLIDE 7

The Death of Just‐in‐Time

Resilience now trades off against efficiency — a deliberate strategic pivot.

Firms moved from single-source, lean models to redundancy-first strategies:

  • Nearshoring and regional diversification to reduce geopolitical exposure.
  • Investment in real-time visibility, inventory buffers, and multi-sourcing.

The Current Landscape: Resilience and Adaptation
Despite the shocks, global trade has
shown resilience. Governments and
firms rerouted suppliers, diversified
markets, and adopted policies to
stabilise supply.

  • Import sourcing shifted toward unaffected regions and alternative suppliers.
  • Longer-term investments target redundancy, security, and strategic stockpiles.

Capacity reallocation and inventory policies reduced disruption risk.

Trade agreements and pragmatic diplomacy stabilized crucial supply corridors.
