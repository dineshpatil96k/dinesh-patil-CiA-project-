Abstract
Russia–Ukraine War and Its Impact on International Trade
The Russia–Ukraine War, which began with Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February
2022, has significantly affected international trade and the global economy. The conflict disrupted
supply chains, increased transportation and energy costs, and created uncertainty in international
markets. Russia is a major exporter of energy products, metals, and fertilizers, while Ukraine is an
important supplier of wheat, corn, sunflower oil, and other agricultural commodities. The war,
along with economic sanctions and trade restrictions, affected the availability and prices of these
essential goods.
This study examines the impact of the Russia–Ukraine War on international trade, focusing on
changes in exports and imports, global supply chains, energy markets, food security, and
transportation costs. It also discusses the effects on developing countries, businesses, and
international trade relations. The conflict encouraged many countries to diversify their suppliers,
explore alternative trade routes, and strengthen domestic production to reduce dependence on
particular regions.
The study concludes that the Russia–Ukraine War has created major challenges for global trade by
increasing economic uncertainty, disrupting traditional trade relationships, and affecting
commodity prices. It also highlights the importance of international cooperation, supply-chain
resilience, and trade diversification in maintaining stability in the global trading system.

Summary
Russia–Ukraine War and Its Impact on International Trade
The Russia–Ukraine War has had a major impact on international trade since Russia’s full-scale
invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Both countries play important roles in global markets.
Russia is a major supplier of crude oil, natural gas, metals, fertilizers, and other raw materials,
while Ukraine is an important exporter of agricultural products such as wheat, corn, sunflower oil,
and other food commodities. The war disrupted the production, transportation, and export of many
of these goods.
Economic sanctions imposed on Russia by several countries changed established trade patterns
and restricted access to certain markets. At the same time, damage to Ukrainian infrastructure,
ports, and transportation networks affected its ability to export goods. These disruptions
contributed to higher prices for energy, food, fertilizers, and some industrial raw materials, creating
difficulties for import-dependent countries.
The conflict also affected global supply chains. Shipping routes were disrupted, insurance and
transportation costs increased, and businesses faced greater uncertainty. Many companies and
countries began looking for alternative suppliers and trade routes to reduce their dependence on
Russia and Ukraine. This resulted in changes in international sourcing and encouraged greater
diversification of supply chains.
Developing and emerging economies were particularly affected by higher food and energy costs.
Countries dependent on imported fuel, fertilizers, and agricultural commodities faced increased
pressure on their trade balances and domestic prices. The conflict also influenced international
trade relationships and encouraged countries to strengthen regional cooperation and domestic
production.
Overall, the Russia–Ukraine War demonstrates how geopolitical conflicts can influence
international trade far beyond the countries directly involved. It has highlighted the importance of
diversified suppliers, secure transportation networks, resilient supply chains, and international
cooperation for maintaining stability in the global trading system.

History and Story of the Russia–Ukraine War and Its Impact on International
Trade
Introduction
The Russia–Ukraine War is a major geopolitical conflict that has had consequences far beyond the
borders of Russia and Ukraine. Although the full-scale war began in February 2022, the conflict
developed over several years, particularly after political and territorial tensions intensified in 2014.
The importance of this conflict to international business comes from the role played by both
countries in global trade. Russia is a major supplier of crude oil, natural gas, coal, fertilizers,
metals and other raw materials, while Ukraine is an important exporter of wheat, maize,
sunflower products, iron ore and other commodities. Consequently, the disruption of
production and trade in the region affected energy markets, food markets, transportation,
manufacturing and global supply chains. The WTO has described Russia and Ukraine as relatively
small shares of overall world output and trade but important suppliers of essential products.

1. Historical Background
Relationship Between Russia and Ukraine
Russia and Ukraine have a long and complicated historical relationship. Ukraine was part of the
Soviet Union until the Soviet Union dissolved in 1991. Ukraine then became an independent
country and developed its own political, economic and foreign-policy relationships.
Over time, Ukraine developed closer economic and political connections with European countries
while maintaining important economic relationships with Russia. This created a complicated
geopolitical situation because Russia considered Ukraine strategically important, while Ukraine
increasingly sought closer integration with European institutions.
The 2013–2014 Political Crisis

Report Page 4

A major turning point occurred in 2013–2014. Ukraine's government under President Viktor
Yanukovych suspended preparations for an association agreement with the European Union,
leading to large demonstrations known as the Euromaidan protests.
The political crisis eventually resulted in Yanukovych leaving office in February 2014. Russia
subsequently took control of Crimea, and a referendum was held there that Russia used as the basis
for annexation. Ukraine and many countries internationally rejected the annexation.
At the same time, fighting began in eastern Ukraine, particularly in the Donetsk and Luhansk
regions, between Ukrainian government forces and Russia-backed separatists. The conflict
continued for years despite attempts at negotiation.

2. The Minsk Agreements
International efforts were made to reduce the fighting in eastern Ukraine.
The Minsk agreements, negotiated in 2014 and 2015, attempted to establish a ceasefire and create
a political framework for resolving the conflict.
However, fighting and political disagreements continued. The situation remained unstable for
several years.
During this period, economic sanctions against Russia were introduced and expanded by several
countries. Trade and investment relationships between Russia and Western economies gradually
became more complicated.

3. Beginning of the Full-Scale War in 2022
On 24 February 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
The conflict rapidly became one of the largest military crises in Europe in decades. Fighting spread
across several parts of Ukraine, while Russia attempted to capture or control strategically
important areas.

Report Page 5

The war caused enormous humanitarian damage and also disrupted economic activity,
transportation infrastructure, ports, factories, agricultural production and international trade.
The international economic response included extensive sanctions and other restrictions affecting
Russia's financial system, technology access, trade and energy-related activities.

4. Why the War Became an International Trade Issue
The conflict became an international trade issue because Russia and Ukraine were deeply
connected to global commodity markets.
Before the war, the two countries were particularly important in:
 Energy exports
 Wheat and grain exports
 Sunflower oil and other agricultural products
 Fertilizers
 Metals
 Industrial raw materials
 Transportation routes between Europe and Asia
According to WTO analysis, Russia and Ukraine together supplied around 25% of global wheat
exports, 15% of barley exports and 45% of sunflower-product exports in 2019. Russia also
accounted for a significant share of global fuel and natural-gas trade.
This meant that when production and transportation were disrupted, the effects were transmitted
to international markets.

5. Impact on International Trade
A. Disruption of Energy Trade
One of the biggest effects was on international energy trade.
Russia has historically been a major exporter of:
 Crude oil
 Natural gas
 Coal
 Refined petroleum products
European countries were particularly dependent on Russian energy supplies.
Following the invasion, sanctions, restrictions and efforts by countries to reduce dependence on
Russian energy changed established trade routes.
European countries increasingly searched for alternative sources of energy. This contributed to
changes in global energy flows.
The World Bank noted that Russia was a major global energy supplier and that disruption to energy
supplies affected production and transportation costs internationally.
Result
Higher energy prices increased costs for:
Oil → Transportation → Manufacturing → Distribution → Consumer prices
Therefore, the effect was not limited to the energy industry.

6. Impact on Food Trade
Ukraine is often described as an important agricultural supplier because of its large agricultural
sector and fertile land.

Report Page 7

Before the war, Ukraine exported large quantities of:
 Wheat
 Maize
 Barley
 Sunflower oil
 Other agricultural commodities
The war disrupted farming, storage, transportation and port operations.
The Black Sea was particularly important because Ukrainian agricultural products traditionally
moved through Black Sea ports.
When shipping was disrupted, importing countries became concerned about food availability and
prices.
UNCTAD reported that the conflict created major challenges for maritime trade and logistics and
increased the need to find alternative transportation routes.

7. Black Sea Grain Initiative
An important international response was the Black Sea Grain Initiative, agreed in July 2022 with
the participation of Russia, Ukraine, Türkiye and the United Nations.
The initiative was designed to facilitate the safe export of Ukrainian grain and other food products
through Black Sea routes.
A separate UN–Russia memorandum addressed facilitating access of Russian food and fertilizer
products to global markets.
These arrangements demonstrated the importance of international cooperation in keeping essential
commodity trade moving during a conflict.

8. Impact on Fertilizer Trade
Russia is an important producer and exporter of fertilizers and fertilizer-related inputs.
Fertilizers are essential for agricultural production. Therefore, disruptions to fertilizer supplies can
affect farmers in countries that depend on imports.
Higher fertilizer and energy costs increased agricultural production expenses in many markets.
This created a chain effect:
War → Energy/Fertilizer disruption → Higher farming costs → Higher food costs →
Pressure on consumers
UNCTAD identified rising food, fuel and fertilizer prices as major channels through which the war
affected developing countries.

9. Impact on Global Supply Chains
The war also exposed weaknesses in global supply chains.
Companies that depended on Russian or Ukrainian suppliers had to find alternatives.
For example, disruptions affected supplies of certain industrial materials. The WTO highlighted
Russia's importance in commodities such as palladium and Ukraine's role in supplying neon used
in some industrial processes.
Businesses therefore began to consider:
 Alternative suppliers
 Different transportation routes
 Higher inventory levels
 Multiple sourcing locations
 Regional manufacturing
 Geopolitical risk management

Report Page 9

This increased the importance of supply-chain resilience.

10. Impact on Shipping and Transportation
The war disrupted transportation through the Black Sea and affected some land and air routes
between Europe and Asia.
Companies faced:
 Higher freight costs
 Higher insurance costs
 Longer delivery times
 Route changes
 Security concerns
 Port disruptions
UNCTAD reported that the conflict increased demand for alternative routes and transportation
infrastructure, while the price of transporting dry bulk goods such as grains increased substantially
during the early months of the war.
This meant that even when goods were available, moving them internationally could become more
expensive.

11. Impact of Sanctions on International Trade
Sanctions became an important part of the international economic response to the invasion.
Various restrictions affected:
 Banking
 Financial transactions

Report Page 10

 Technology
 Investment
 Energy
 Transportation
 Selected imports and exports
These restrictions changed the traditional trading relationships between Russia and many Western
economies.
However, sanctions did not mean that all Russian international trade stopped. Instead, some trade
flows were redirected toward other markets.
For example, WTO analysis found that Russia's exports increased in value in 2022, partly because
of higher commodity prices, while its export destinations changed. China and India became more
important destinations for Russian exports, while exports to some Western markets declined.

12. Impact on Developing Countries
Developing countries were particularly vulnerable to increases in food, fuel and fertilizer prices.
Many developing economies depend heavily on imported commodities. When international prices
increase, governments and consumers may face greater financial pressure.
UNCTAD warned that developing and least-developed countries were particularly exposed to the
effects of increased commodity prices, trade costs and financial volatility.
The major problems included:
 Higher food prices
 Higher fuel costs
 Higher fertilizer costs
 Inflation

 Increased import bills
 Pressure on foreign-exchange reserves
 Higher transportation costs

13. Impact on India
The Russia–Ukraine War also had important implications for India.
India is a major importer of crude oil and an important participant in global agricultural and
manufacturing markets.
The changes in global energy markets affected India's import costs. At the same time, India
increased purchases of Russian crude oil after the beginning of the war, while Indian companies
had to manage changing shipping, payment, insurance and sanctions-related conditions.
The conflict also created opportunities for some Indian exporters because companies and countries
were searching for alternative suppliers.
For Indian businesses, the conflict highlighted the importance of:
 Diversifying export markets
 Finding alternative suppliers
 Monitoring international sanctions
 Managing currency and freight risks
 Maintaining multiple logistics options
 Understanding geopolitical developments

14. Changes in International Trade Patterns
One of the most important long-term effects of the war has been the reorganization of trade
relationships.

Report Page 12

Countries and companies increasingly began considering not only:
"Where can we buy the product at the lowest cost?"
but also:
"Is this supply chain reliable and politically secure?"
This has encouraged:
Trade Diversification
Businesses seek suppliers from several countries rather than depending on one source.
Regionalization
Some companies consider locating production closer to their major markets.
Supply-Chain Resilience
Companies maintain alternative suppliers and logistics routes.
Energy Diversification
Countries seek multiple sources of oil, gas and renewable energy.
Strategic Stockpiling
Governments and companies may maintain reserves of strategically important products.

15. International Trade Was Disrupted, But It Did Not Stop
An important point for a research project is that the war did not completely stop international
trade.
Initially, there were fears of a much larger disruption to global trade. However, WTO analysis
found that international trade proved more resilient than many early expectations suggested.
Countries affected by shortages were able in many cases to switch to alternative suppliers.
For example, the WTO reported that Ukraine's exports fell substantially in 2022, while Russia's
exports increased in value, largely because of higher prices for fuels, fertilizers and cereals.

Report Page 13

This demonstrates an important feature of international trade: when one supplier becomes
unavailable, global markets can sometimes adjust by shifting supply to other countries.

17. Conclusion
The history of the Russia–Ukraine War shows how geopolitical tensions can gradually develop
into a major international economic crisis. The conflict that intensified in 2014 eventually became
a full-scale war in February 2022, creating severe humanitarian, political and economic
consequences.
From an international-business perspective, one of the most important lessons is that geopolitical
events can directly influence international trade. The war affected energy supplies, agricultural
exports, fertilizer markets, transportation networks, industrial inputs and financial relationships.
The conflict also demonstrated the interconnected nature of the global economy. A disruption in
one region can affect commodity prices, production costs, shipping expenses and consumer
markets across the world. UNCTAD and the WTO both documented significant effects on food
security, energy markets, supply chains and trade costs.
At the same time, international trade showed considerable ability to adapt. Countries searched for
alternative suppliers, companies redesigned supply chains, and trade routes changed. The WTO
found that trade remained more resilient than some early forecasts suggested.
Therefore, the Russia–Ukraine War is an important case study for international business,
international trade, globalization and supply-chain management. It demonstrates the need for
businesses and countries to consider geopolitical risk, diversify their markets and suppliers, and
develop flexible supply chains capable of responding to unexpected global disruptions.
